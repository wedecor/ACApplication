## The single most-misunderstood AC service

If you've ever been told "your AC needs a gas top-up every year", you've
been mis-sold to. Refrigerant gas is in a sealed, closed-loop system.
It does not get consumed during normal operation. A new, correctly
installed unit should run 7-10 years before any refill is needed.

When a refill **is** needed, it's because there's a **leak** somewhere
in the system — a flare-nut joint, a hairline crack in the copper, a
damaged Schrader valve. **The leak must be found and patched before the
refill**, or the new gas will leak out within months.

## How to tell if you genuinely need a refill

Three signs that point at low refrigerant rather than a different fault:

1. **Frost on part of the indoor coil** — partial frosting is a charge
   problem; uniform frost is usually airflow.
2. **Outdoor copper pipes are warm, not cold** — both pipes should be
   cold to the touch when cooling.
3. **Cooling drops the longer the AC runs** — instead of stabilising
   the temperature drifts up after 30 minutes.

Things that **look** like a refill need but usually aren't:

- Filter is clogged (rinse and re-test).
- Outdoor unit is in full sun (add a sunshade).
- Drain line is blocked and float-switch tripped.
- Indoor coil is dirty (chemical wash, not a refill).

Reject any quote that:

- Doesn't include **electronic leak-detection**.
- Doesn't run a **vacuum pulldown** before the refill.
- Doesn't share a **before/after pressure reading** on WhatsApp.

## How to make a refill last

After a refill is done correctly, you can stretch the life of the
charge by 12-18 months with three habits:

- **Service every six months.** A dirty coil makes the compressor work
  harder and stresses the joints.
- **Don't run below 22 °C continuously.** Continuous low setpoints
  cause coil icing and the resulting thermal shock damages flare-nut
  joints.
- **Get the install audited every 3 years.** Flare-nuts loosen over time;
  a 30-minute torque-check is cheap insurance.
