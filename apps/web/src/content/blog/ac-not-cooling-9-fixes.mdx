Most "AC not cooling" calls turn out to be one of nine common issues — and the
first four are things you can check yourself in under ten minutes. Walk through
this list before booking a technician.

## 1. The filter is clogged

The single most common cause of weak cooling. A clogged mesh filter chokes
airflow and the unit cycles harder for less output. Switch the AC off,
remove the front panel, slide out the filter and rinse under tap water.

> **When to escalate.** If the filter looks clean but cooling is still
> weak, the inner coil and blower wheel are likely caked in bio-film —
> that needs a chemical deep-clean, not a DIY rinse.

## 2. Wrong mode

A surprising number of "broken" ACs are simply in **Fan** or **Dry** mode.
Confirm the remote is set to **Cool** with a target of 22–24 °C. If your
remote has an "Eco" or "Sleep" mode, those throttle the compressor.

## 3. Thermostat sensor obstructed

The indoor air-sensor sits behind the front panel. If a coat hanger or
curtain is pressed against it, the AC thinks the room is already cool and
shuts the compressor down. Move the obstruction and wait two minutes.

## 4. The outdoor unit is in direct sun

Outdoor units (condensers) dump heat into the air around them. If yours
sits on a hot west-facing balcony in full sun, cooling efficiency drops
by 20–30 %. A simple sunshade — leaving at least 30 cm of clearance —
restores most of that capacity.

## 5. The refrigerant is low

If steps 1–4 don't help, your unit may be low on gas. Two quick tests:

1. Press your hand against the copper pipes outside. Both should be cold;
   one is warm → low gas or a leak.
2. Check the indoor coil after 15 minutes of running. Frost build-up on
   only part of the coil → low charge.

Refilling refrigerant gas is **not** a DIY job — it requires recovery
equipment and electronic leak-detection. Book a [gas-refill service](/services/ac-repair).

## 6. The capacitor is failing

If the outdoor unit hums but the fan doesn't spin, or trips the MCB on
startup, the start capacitor is on its way out. Average capacitor
replacement: ₹600–1,100 for window units, ₹900–1,800 for splits.

## 7. The drain line is blocked

A backed-up drain line can trip the float switch and shut cooling off
entirely. You'll see water around the indoor unit. Pour a cup of warm
water into the drain pan — if it doesn't drain, the line needs flushing.

## 8. The remote is failing

Lift your phone camera up to the remote sensor and press a button. You
should see the IR LED flash on screen. If not — the remote, not the AC,
is dead. Buy a universal remote (₹200–400) and verify before calling for
service.

## 9. The compressor is dying

If the outdoor unit clicks but never starts, or runs in 30-second
bursts, you may need a hard-start kit (₹800–1,500) or, in worst case, a
compressor replacement (₹6,000–18,000 depending on tonnage). At that
price point, weigh repair vs. replacement carefully.
